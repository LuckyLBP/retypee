How to replace text in a PDF

  1. Open the PDF.

    Drop it on the page.

  2. Press Ctrl+F (Cmd+F on a Mac).

    Type the text you are looking for and what it should be replaced with. A counter shows how many matches there are, and on which pages.

  3. Click Replace all.

    Every match changes. If you are not happy, Undo brings everything back, and when it looks right you download the file.

Options that help you hit the right text

  • Match case – tell “Invoice” from “invoice”.
  • Whole words – change “2026” but not “20261”.
  • Squeeze – shrinks longer replacement text so it fits on the line.

Common uses

  • Change an invoice, quote or order number that repeats on every page.
  • Swap a city, date or year in a template document.
  • Update a company name or address in a contract you wrote yourself.

Good to know

  • A match has to sit on one line in one text style. Text split across lines or styles is skipped.
  • If you only want to change a single occurrence, click that line instead.

Questions about find and replace in a PDF

Can you find and replace in a PDF?

Yes. In Retypee you press Ctrl+F (Cmd+F on a Mac), type what you are looking for and what to replace it with, and click Replace all.

Does the formatting change when text is replaced?

The new text gets the same baseline, font, size and colour as the text it replaces. If the text is right-aligned, such as an amount in a table, the right edge is kept.

Can I undo a replacement?

Yes. Undo (Ctrl+Z) brings back the whole replacement in one step, and Redo does it again.

How many matches can I replace at once?

All of them in the document. Retypee shows the number and the pages they are on before you replace.
